Stanford's One Hundred Year Study on Artificial Intelligence (AI100), which reports every five years, released its third report, [AI Goes Mainstream: The Scaling Era and the Rise of Generative AI](https://ai100.stanford.edu/reports/2026-report). The report has twelve takeaways from an expert study panel. The panel finds that the formula behind the last five years' gains — ever more computing power and data — is hitting limits, as high-quality data runs short and frontier compute grows expensive, so progress will depend more on smarter algorithms and more reasoning at the point of use. Record spending on data centres and chips has raised bubble fears and strained energy and freshwater supplies, yet AI "is not yet paying its way": its impact does not yet show in macroeconomic data or company returns. For science, AI is compressing research timelines from years to months or weeks and AI-powered labs may be the norm within a decade, but machine-generated discoveries will need principled validation. The panel also finds that the public has had little say in how AI is built, and that governance is fragmented and framed around national security, with export controls used as tools of economic competition and international summits producing principles rather than binding steps.
