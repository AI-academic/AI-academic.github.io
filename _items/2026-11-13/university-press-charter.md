Seven university presses — Cambridge and Bristol as co-leads, with Edinburgh, Liverpool, Manchester, Princeton and University of London Press — launched the University Press Charter during University Press Week (9–13 November), describing it as the first collective commitment by university presses to shape the future of scholarly publishing. Hosted by the Association of University Presses, the charter has 108 signatories from 26 countries, including Canada. Signatories commit to putting quality ahead of volume, transparency about publishing services and choices, wider global participation, fair recognition for scholarly contributions, and responsible approaches to AI that keep human judgment at the centre. The AI commitment covers preserving scholarly accountability, copyright-compliant licensing deals, responsible management of licensed content used by AI systems, and attribution of original sources where practical.
