The MIT AI Risk Initiative has updated the severity scale in its AI Incident Tracker, which classifies incidents reported to the public AI Incident Database. Each incident is now rated across 13 harm categories on five levels from Negligible to Catastrophic. Three categories are new — psychological harm, epistemic harm (damage to knowledge and information) and child sexual exploitation and abuse. Across 1,785 incidents, epistemic harm is the most frequently recorded category, mostly at Minor severity, followed by psychological harm, while privacy and child-exploitation incidents are more often rated Substantial or Severe. The ratings are produced by a large language model pipeline rather than human coders; the team says its agreement with expert consensus is comparable to agreement between two human experts, but the supporting paper is not yet published. The team also cautions that voluntary reporting skews the sample, and that rating incidents one at a time misses harms that build up across many events, such as labor displacement.
