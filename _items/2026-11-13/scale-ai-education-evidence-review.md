Stanford's SCALE Initiative updated its review of causal research — randomized trials and quasi-experimental studies — on generative AI in education, adding papers published between October 2025 and June 2026. The central finding is that AI is more likely to help when students use it to understand material than when it produces work for them, and that offloading academic work can lower later performance without AI. Tool design and implementation appear to matter more than tool type, and general-purpose chatbots did not consistently do worse than purpose-built educational tools. On the teaching side, AI cut lesson-preparation time without a detected drop in quality in one study, while another found a broad teaching-support tool lowered student motivation and confidence. Most studies are un-reviewed preprints, and the authors caution against generalizing the findings.
