A Nature Medicine study tested an explainable AI tool combining blood tests, CT scans, tissue samples and genetic data to predict which advanced non-small-cell lung cancer patients respond to immunotherapy. A blood-based AI model modestly outperformed the standard biomarker used today. The larger effect came from the humans: when doctors used the tool's explanations to inform their judgement, their rate of correctly identifying patients who would respond rose from 0.72 to 0.87. The modest standalone gain alongside the large assisted gain is the argument for evaluating these systems as decision support rather than as a replacement biomarker.
