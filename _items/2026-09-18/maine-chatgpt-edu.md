The University of Maine System rolled out ChatGPT Edu to all students and staff for the 2026-27 academic year under a two-year contract costing just under US$1.4 million, which works out to roughly $22 per user per year. Adoption is voluntary and no mandate is planned. Earth and climate science professor Alice Bernosky said in-person exams remain a setting where AI has no place, while the tool can assist with brainstorming and structuring work. Ryan Low, the system's chancellor for finance and AI integration, emphasised that user data is not used to train public models, presenting this as a security advantage over free consumer tools. At that per-seat price the licence is cheap enough that the real cost of the decision sits in support and assessment redesign rather than in the contract.
