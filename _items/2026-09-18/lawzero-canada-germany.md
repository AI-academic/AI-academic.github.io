Canada and Germany announced joint funding for LawZero, the Montréal-based AI safety non-profit founded by Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio and incubated at Mila. The funding consists of C$150 million from Canada for research and engineering talent and computing capacity, and €100 million from Germany for research and development. The money supports Scientist AI, described as a system designed to reason transparently and produce reliable, evidence-based outputs without goals of its own, explicitly distinguished from autonomous frontier systems. Canadian firms Hypertec and 5C will provide computational capacity. The project is expected to create 360 full-time Canadian positions. Notably, the release does not name Mila or any university as a formal party. It is interesting to note that a non-profit will now compete for the same senior AI researchers universities are trying to recruit.
