A study in NEJM AI, highlighted by Nature, found that care recommended by general-purpose AI systems cost on average 3.2 times as much as the care physicians actually documented, priced at US Medicare rates. The gap came mainly from tests and specialist referrals. In two-thirds of visits the physician ordered no tests, while every AI system suggested some. Specialized medical AI systems were no cheaper, and prompting the systems to be cost-aware or sparing reduced the excess but did not remove it.
