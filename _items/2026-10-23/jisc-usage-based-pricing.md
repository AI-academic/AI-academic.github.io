Jisc's Rebecca Flook sets out how frontier AI vendors are shifting from flat-rate subscriptions to pricing based on usage, citing GitHub Copilot's move to credit-based pricing. Output typically costs four to six times as much as input, non-English text consumes more, and although the price per unit has fallen sharply since 2023, total usage is growing faster; agentic AI raises consumption without adding paid seats. In a worked example, an organization licensing Microsoft 365 Copilot for 500 staff doubles its annual bill by adding the agentic Copilot Cowork for 100 of them, with no ceiling if they use it more than expected. Flook argues larger institutions will negotiate better terms, widening the gap between institutions, and recommends building six controls into procurement from the start: clear budget ownership, real-time usage monitoring, agreed usage limits, transparent contract terms, role-based controls and reasonable exit terms. Jisc is supporting sector discussions that will lead to a sector statement.
