A Northeastern University database counts more than 100 US universities offering an AI major within a computer science department, nearly double the number a year earlier; a Northeastern study in May found that more than a third of the institutions surveyed require an ethics course in the major. In Massachusetts, Bentley University's AI major has grown from a handful of students in fall 2024 to about 100 undergraduates; MIT's "AI and Decision Making" major, launched in 2022, is now the second most popular on campus; WPI's new AI degree, which began enrolling this fall, requires a responsible-AI course; and Northeastern has renamed its data science bachelor's degree to AI. Administrators cite student anxiety about an AI-driven job market as the driver, with Bentley president Brent Chrite saying "we worry about students from other universities who might be better trained in AI."
