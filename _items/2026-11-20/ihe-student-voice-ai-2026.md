Inside Higher Ed's annual Student Voice survey of more than 5,000 undergraduates at 310 US colleges, conducted in October, finds AI now routine for about four in five students, and about a third say it helps them study more efficiently and take on hard concepts with more confidence. The trade-offs are showing too: three in ten rely less on professors and tutoring centers, one in six say they lean on AI too heavily to think or write on their own, and a quarter have changed how they write to avoid being flagged by AI detectors. Asked what responsible use should look like, students put two things first. One, AI should help them learn rather than do the work for them (65%), and two, there should be transparency about when and how AI is used (60%).
