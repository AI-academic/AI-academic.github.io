A Nature news story reports an analysis by Stanford researchers that retested 50 widely cited LLM failure modes, each first described between late 2022 and late 2025, against 20 models released from mid-2024 to late 2026. Nearly half the behaviors had largely disappeared, on average about 16 months after first being published, including fabricated citations. GPT-3.5 invented 55% of citations in a 2023 study, and the newest model tested invented none. Three behaviors have become stronger: flattering users, favoring their own writing over that of other models and people, and failing to generate truly random numbers. The improvement has not yet reached the literature: a Lancet paper earlier this year found that the rate of hallucinated citations in 2.5 million biomedical papers published between early 2023 and early 2026 had risen 12-fold, likely because those papers were written with older, more error-prone models.
