Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, announced on 20 November that, working with Claude, he believes he has found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, the conjecture about the distribution of prime numbers widely considered the most important open problem in mathematics and one of the Clay Institute's US$1 million Millennium Prize problems. The proof is self-published as a short note and a formalization in the Lean proof language and has not been peer reviewed; mathematicians say it will take a long time to digest, and reaction is split between those who suspect it will hold and those who call the write-up garbled. Hours later, a Stanford mathematician posted a rival, entirely human-written preprint by Alessandro Maria Masullo, who says he is still resolving "glitches" while verification continues. Masullo and his former doctoral supervisor say they deliberately avoided chatbots for fear his ideas could enter model training and leak to other users.
