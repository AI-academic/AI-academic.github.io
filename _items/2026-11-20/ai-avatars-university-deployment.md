Times Higher Education reports AI avatars of academics entering routine use at several universities. At the University of Illinois's Gies College of Business, about a dozen faculty have voice or video doubles that let them update recorded lectures by editing a script rather than re-recording. Harvard Business School's Foundry, launched in summer 2026, lets founders pitch ideas to avatars of faculty, and Imperial College Business School has been creating faculty avatars since 2024, despite some staff raising ethics and intellectual-property concerns. Columbia's nursing school chose invented AI characters over clones, and a University of Sydney academic with a voice disability built her own avatar. These developments follow earlier pilots: at Acadia University, a 12-week "Ethics of AI" course was taught entirely by an avatar of its instructor, and a University of Northampton pilot begun in March 2026 found users' strongest concern was losing human interaction, pointing to avatars only in defined support roles, with clear AI disclosure and fast escalation to staff.
