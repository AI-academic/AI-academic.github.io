In his latest notes, Bill Gates argues that AI will be unusually fast and disruptive because of its ability to substitute for human cognition, spread quickly through computers and internet, and requiring very little training to start using it. The technology could either equalize humans regardless of their location and place of work or become a major source of injustice. He also argues that governments need to start preparing for it very soon. 

The main points in his notes are:
- Major risks of 1) permanent job displacement across sectors and at different levels; 2) increased cybercrime, disinformation, surveillance, bioterrorism, and other types of harms; and 3) damage to children’s development, relationships among humans, and critical-thinking skills.
- Major benefits that are likely to be gained due to rapid scientific discovery, better healthcase and agriculture, better public services, and education that can be personalized to individual needs. However, these gains will not occur everywhere without governments putting efforts and philanthropic investment.  
- proposals for new policies such as the establishment of national and international institutions that can coordinate AI governance; setting aside some role for humans termed as 'Human Reserved', especially in areas where care, dignity, and judgement are essential; and taxing AI use and robots to slow down replacement of workers while using funds to retrain people and create stronger social proections. 

For universities, Gate's message is that AI should support learning while preserving 'productive struggle', critical thinking, and meaningful human roles. His note also implies that universities will be central to workforce transition, interdisciplinary governance, research acceleration, and the public debate over which AI-related decisions and functions should be managed by humans.
