A report published by the University of Minnesota's College of Education and Human Development asked 30 learning scientists and education researchers to assess learning processes across K-20 education. 

The panel saw the strongest potential of AI in practice activities such as feedback, self-testing, tutoring and scaffolding, while identifying risks to memory formation, higher-order thinking, self-regulation, social learning and writing, the one domain-specific item the experts agreed is at high risk. The authors judge the existing empirical literature on generative AI and learning to be seriously lacking in rigor. The report is an expert response rather than a systematic review or controlled study.
