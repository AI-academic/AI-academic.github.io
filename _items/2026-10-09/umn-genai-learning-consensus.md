A report published by the University of Minnesota's College of Education and Human Development asked 30 learning scientists and education researchers to assess 65 learning processes across K-20 education; 42 reached the report's consensus threshold for enhancement, vulnerability or both. The panel saw the strongest potential in practice activities such as feedback, self-testing, tutoring and scaffolding, while identifying risks to memory formation, higher-order thinking, self-regulation, social learning and writing, the one domain-specific item the experts agreed is at high risk. The authors judge the existing empirical literature on generative AI and learning to be seriously lacking in rigour. The report is expert elicitation rather than a systematic review or controlled study.
