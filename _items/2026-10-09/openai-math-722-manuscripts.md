On October 6, OpenAI posted 722 manuscripts produced by an internal frontier model to GitHub, claiming progress on 372 mathematics problems, with Lean formalizations of many proofs and protocols for revisions and citations; a day later it withdrew three because of a sign error and revised 14 others. OpenAI says it drew on advice from the Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence hosted at the Institute for Advanced Study, whose guidelines for AI labs, issued in late September, asked them to stop testing advanced mathematical problems on proprietary models; the group says it is up to mathematicians to judge how far those guidelines were followed. The Association for Human Mathematics urged colleagues to stop working with OpenAI, while Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge called it "the most significant moment in mathematical history." OpenAI also plans to fund workshops and programs on understanding AI-produced results. What is established is structural: a company can now produce and post research-scale mathematics in bulk. Whether those results are correct is not established, and the checking burden falls on academic reviewers.
