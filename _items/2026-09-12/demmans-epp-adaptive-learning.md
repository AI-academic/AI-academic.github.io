CIFAR published a feature on Carrie Demmans Epp, Canada CIFAR AI Chair at Amii and associate professor of computing science at the University of Alberta, covering a two-month pilot of adaptive machine-learning software in rural Alberta K-12 classrooms. All participating students showed measurable improvement, and students with the lowest cognitive-ability scores showed no summer learning loss, with September scores exceeding June scores; the school division has since expanded the program. Demmans Epp cautions against "offloading everything to the system" and calls for rigorous bias testing before automated essay-grading tools are deployed. He notes that Canadian classrooms average 22 to 26 students with some K-6 classes reaching 40 to 60. Those class sizes are both the argument for the tools and the reason the bias question cannot be deferred until after adoption.
