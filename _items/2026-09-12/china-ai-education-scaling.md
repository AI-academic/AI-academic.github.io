An analysis by Tsinghua University's Institute for Artificial Intelligence in Education assesses China's national "AI + Education" Action Plan. Tsinghua expanded from 8 AI-enabled courses in autumn 2023 to 451 by spring 2026 and now shares "knowledge engines" with 80 partner institutions, while Fudan has built a 116-course AI system spanning all students and disciplines. A national platform had identified 80 exemplar cases by October 2025, alongside a free National Smart Education Platform and a planned shared computing-service platform intended to lower adoption costs for less-resourced universities. The argument running through it is that scaling educational improvement depends on regional universities adapting rather than imitating what flagship institutions have built — the same distance between the exemplar and the system that national AI strategies elsewhere tend to leave unaddressed.
