A declaration signed by 25 Fields Medalists objects to AI companies' approach to mathematical problem-solving, raising four specific harms: 1) the loss of conceptual understanding where problems function as "landmarks and lighthouses"; 2) attribution and plagiarism risk; 3) disruption to student training; and 4) the dependence of mathematical knowledge on "willing mathematicians who must take care of their development and integration into the mathematical canon." It follows the Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics, issued in June 2026, which calls for disclosure of automated tool use, rigorous attribution, proof-based verification, author rights over training data, and publication through peer review rather than press release. The objection is less about whether machines can do the mathematics than about what a discipline loses when results arrive without the understanding, and without the people who would have developed it.
