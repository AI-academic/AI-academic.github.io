Anthropic's economics team published an interactive model setting out three scenarios for how AI could reshape the US economy by 2030. In the mildest: the economy grows 1.6%, wages hold steady, and little else changes. In the middle scenario, AI does half of all knowledge work on its own. The economy grows 8.3%, unemployment edges up to around 5%, and wages for office and professional workers stop rising while other workers gain. In the most extreme scenario, the economy grows 32.4% but knowledge-worker wages fall by more than 10%, unemployment reaches levels normally seen in a serious recession, and a far larger share of national income flows to the owners of businesses and technology rather than to people's pay. A companion survey of more than 10,000 Americans found expectations clustered around the middle scenario, with roughly one in ten expecting the extreme one. The authors mention that the model ignores government policy responses, business cycles and knock-on effects on consumer demand, and that its estimates of what displacement would cost workers are "very coarse", which is a caveat when using these numbers into any planning documents.
