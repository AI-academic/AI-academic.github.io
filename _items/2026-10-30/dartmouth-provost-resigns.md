Santiago Schnell resigned as Dartmouth provost on Wednesday 28 October, less than two weeks before a planned faculty no-confidence vote. A committee convened by President Sian Leah Beilock reviewed his Dartmouth Claude account logs and his laptop, did not use AI detectors, and did not receive logs from his personal AI accounts. Among other things, it found that for a forthcoming article on academic freedom Schnell asked Claude whether the text showed signs of AI writing and then revised using Claude's suggestions. The case began in August 2026, when *Semafor* reported that the detector Pangram flagged his 9 August *Washington Post* op-ed as at least 80% AI-generated; in September *The Dartmouth* reported a Pangram score of 96% across nine of his 2026 articles. Beilock said Dartmouth wants to stay at the forefront of AI and that leaders must model transparency.
