Technion is opening the year with a record intake of about 2,400 new undergraduates. Before classes began, it ran "AI Days," a ten-day series of dozens of workshops, lectures, hackathons and hands-on challenges giving students, researchers and both academic and administrative staff practical tools for using AI in learning, teaching, research, engineering development and laboratory management. Prof. Benny Kimelfeld, Dean of Undergraduate Studies, said the goal was for the community to "begin the semester with new, up-to-date skills." A Technion report from August says a steering committee is being formed for long-term AI-in-courses strategy, that mandatory AI literacy mini-courses for all students are starting, that the campus has more than 1,000 GPUs, and that Israel's National AI Program contributed 5,000 GPUs for academic research.
