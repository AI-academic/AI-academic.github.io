Researchers of Harvard's Institute for Quantitative Social Science report that AI-generated article reviews are "now better, by every measure we could construct" than reviews from well-compensated human experts. The comparison used pre-submission drafts from rapidPeer, a Harvard review service the authors run, and they note that the result cuts against that interest. AI reviews came from Claude Opus 4.8, in a matched condition without tools and a tuned condition with web retrieval. GPT-5.5 judged the pairs, and separately three former editors of leading journals each judged ten. The AI judge favored AI reviews in 132 of 132 decided pairs in the tuned condition, and the human editors favoured AI in 26 of 30 judgments (87%). An AI audit flagged 5.5% of AI claims as factually incorrect against 15.9% of human claims, and none of the 651 DOI or arXiv identifiers cited by the AI was fabricated. The authors report fourteen robustness checks, none of which changed the conclusion.
