In a peer-reviewed study published August 24 and captured in today's short look-back, researchers analyzed 194,631 cross-sectional social-science articles using an LLM-based workflow with human validation. They reported that 46% contained causal language in titles or abstracts across 1980-2024, rising from 20% in 2000 to 60% in 2024. Experiments with five LLMs found that summaries could remove hedges or introduce unqualified causal claims, while prompts emphasizing caution reduced the effect. The authors also found that methodological labels and associational wording reduced causal interpretation among human readers.
