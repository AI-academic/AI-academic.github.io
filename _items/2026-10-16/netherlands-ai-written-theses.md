RTL Nieuws ran hundreds of recent master's theses from 12 Dutch universities through the Pangram detector, which revealed about 6% as almost entirely AI-generated, 14% as more than half AI text, 39% as under half, and 41% as showing none. This means roughly one in five were mostly chatbot-written. Nearly half of the heavily AI-written theses carried an AI-use declaration, mostly claiming only brainstorming or proofreading, and at least two students who declared no AI use had theses scored at 75% and 90% AI. None of the 12 universities, nor Universities of the Netherlands (UNL), tracks AI-related thesis fraud, and their policies differ. Utrecht bans detection software on student work and Leiden does not allow professors to use such tools. Education Minister Rianne Letschert called it fraud and is to present a strategy before the end of the year. UNL responded that a detector score alone cannot establish guilt and that the findings raise questions about how final assignments are designed.
