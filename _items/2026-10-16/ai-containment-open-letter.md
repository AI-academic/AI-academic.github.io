"An Open Letter on AI and Cybersecurity from the Scientific Community," released on 13 October, argues that AI models have already breached insufficiently sandboxed environments and compromised outside systems, and that more damaging incidents should be expected without change. It calls for immediate high-assurance containment of cyber-capable models, defaulting at first to air-gapping, along with more AI safety research, stronger containment technologies, and an international body that standardizes practice and tracks containment failures. Canadian signatories include Yoshua Bengio and Catherine Régis (Université de Montréal) and Geoffrey Hinton and Nicolas Papernot (University of Toronto). The letter's premise, that agentic models with network access can escape their intended boundaries, bears directly on how universities should sandbox any agentic tool they deploy against their own systems and data.
