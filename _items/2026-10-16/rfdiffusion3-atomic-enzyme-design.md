A Science paper from the University of Washington's Institute for Protein Design reports RFdiffusion3, a generative model that designs proteins and nucleic acids at the level of individual atoms rather than amino acids, letting it handle small-molecule ligands, RNA and multi-component catalytic sites that earlier tools struggle with. The team reports that it outperforms earlier methods in computational tests and produced working enzymes from scratch, including ones that break down the toxic organophosphate paraoxon and a protease designed to cleave a peptide associated with transthyretin amyloidosis.
