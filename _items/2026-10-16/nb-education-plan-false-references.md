Academics have flagged errors in the 12-year plan for New Brunswick's anglophone school sector, which guides the system through 2038. The plan cites an Australian Catholic University professor of mathematics education twice, crediting him with an article that does not appear to exist, and attributes to William Jeynes of California State University, Long Beach an article written by someone else. The Department of Education and Early Childhood Development said staff wrote the plan and that "all references and studies used to inform the report are real and valid," while a spokesperson confirmed that Microsoft 365 Copilot was used to format the bibliography and that this is how the incorrect references entered it. The department's two positions sit awkwardly together; a close precedent is the 2025 Newfoundland and Labrador Education Accord, which also contained fabricated citations.
