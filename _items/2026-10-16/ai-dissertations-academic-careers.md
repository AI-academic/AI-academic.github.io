An NBER working paper, reported by Nature, used the Pangram detector to measure AI-generated writing in US STEM doctoral dissertations from 2019 to 2026. AI writing is absent before 2023 and appears in 29% of dissertations filed in 2026; in 2026 abstracts it reaches 36% in mathematics and computer science, 35% in engineering, 26% in life sciences and 22% in physical sciences. Use is higher among non-native English speakers and at lower-ranked programs. Within the same program and cohort, graduates whose dissertations contain AI writing are less likely to enter academic research and more likely to take non-tenure-track or industry roles, most strongly among US-origin students. The study is not yet peer reviewed and the career finding is correlational, but the measured scale of AI writing in dissertations bears directly on supervision practice, thesis regulations and examiner guidance.
