Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, with Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation Evan Solomon and the Federation of Canadian Municipalities, released a five-part national framework: create lasting local benefits, protect electricity ratepayers from cost shifts, minimize water use and environmental impacts, maintain transparency about local impacts, and deliver strategic value to Canada. The principles are voluntary and create no new federal review process; they are positioned as a baseline complementing existing provincial, territorial, municipal and Indigenous regulatory processes. Twenty-three organizations signed on, among them AWS, Anthropic, Bell, Cohere, Cologix, eStruxture, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and TELUS. The release ties the framework to the "AI for All" national AI strategy and the Canadian Sovereign AI Compute Strategy. It arrives as public opinion sours on the buildout: polling cited by BetaKit finds more than two-thirds of Canadians oppose data centres being built near their homes, and Solomon describes the principles as a tool for local authorities rather than a federal enforcement mechanism.
