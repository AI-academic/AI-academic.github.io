Retraction Watch's weekend round-up consolidates the week's research-integrity discourse in one place: a preprint documenting how language models invent researcher names and affiliations in scholarly databases, an appeals court upholding constitutional protections for research-fraud whistleblowers, and the retraction of a Dan Ariely paper after sleuths flagged signs of data tampering. The same round-up notes Chinese authorities investigating more than 40 researchers and further retractions over calculation errors and fabricated findings. Taken together the items show AI-related integrity questions moving from commentary into retraction records and litigation, which is where they start to carry institutional consequences.
