A Canadian Press feature reports teachers, administrators and researchers describing AI guidance in Canadian schools as fragmented across provinces and boards, the product of rapid deployment alongside a federal push for broad accessibility. Alberta Teachers' Association president Jason Schilling says it "feels a little bit like the Wild West"; Manitoba education minister Tracy Schmidt says her department is drafting guiding principles for thoughtful, ethical and safe decisions; McGill's Adam Dubé argues the federal government should help regulate educational AI products sold in Canada. The story concerns the K-12 system rather than the university sector, but the argument for a coordinated national framework is the same one the Council of Ontario Universities made provincially in May 2026.
