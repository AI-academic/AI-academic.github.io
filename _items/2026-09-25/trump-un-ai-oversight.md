Speaking at the UN General Assembly on September 22, President Trump rejected international oversight of AI as a "globalist scheme" and likened warnings that AI will kill us all to climate alarm. He said he was "not going to stifle growth of something that will be bigger than the industrial revolution," having earlier called existential AI risk a "hoax." The speech came ten days after Anthropic's chief executive, Dario Amodei, proposed that frontier labs slow the pace of model improvement and give independent evaluators full access, a call OpenAI's Sam Altman has backed; both were due to brief the UN Security Council on AI and international security on September 25. Much of the independent evaluation of frontier models happens at universities and research institutes, and it remains unclear what role academic safety researchers will have, or whether that work will be governed by an international arrangement or by voluntary agreements with vendors.
