After Semafor flagged Provost Santiago Schnell's August Washington Post op-ed on AI cheating as AI-written, the student paper The Dartmouth ran his other published writing through the detection tool Pangram. Nine pieces from 2026 came back with a median score of 96% AI-written. The email he sent the paper to explain himself was flagged as well. Schnell says he uses AI only for "language refinement, copy-editing, improving clarity and organization," and has since said he should always disclose it in his public writing. Dartmouth's AAUP chapter is circulating a petition calling for his removal, and President Sian Leah Beilock has announced a review of his AI use. The episode is awkward for a college whose president has argued publicly that Dartmouth needs more AI, not less.
