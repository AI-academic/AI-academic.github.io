Anthropic has introduced a life-sciences research group with its own Bay Area wet lab, where human scientists do all the bench work. By the company's account, about 950 Claude agents searched more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases in 21 hours and turned up a previously undescribed enzyme system with CRISPR-like repeats, which it calls array-associated reverse transcriptases. The lab has expressed and characterised the protein, but its biological function is still unknown, and the work is a preprint that has not been peer reviewed. Outside reaction was mixed: a Stanford bioengineer praised the pattern-finding, while a Washington University microbiologist saw nothing yet to suggest a rival to CRISPR as a technology. Anthropic's chief executive, Dario Amodei, has acknowledged that Stanford researchers had earlier found a system that is in some ways similar.
