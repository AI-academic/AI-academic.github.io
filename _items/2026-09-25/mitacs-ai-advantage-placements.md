The federal government is putting up to $162 million over five years, starting in 2026–27, into 10,000 co-funded AI work placements for post-secondary students, recent graduates and postdoctoral fellows. The money runs through Mitacs AI Advantage in two streams: ADOPT, which pairs businesses with academic researchers to test and deploy AI, and AI+X, which co-funds research placements applying AI in fields such as agriculture and health care. It falls under the national AI strategy, AI for All, which aims for 90,000 AI-related jobs and placements by 2031. Mitacs chief executive Stephen Lucas described the approach as "embedding young, highly skilled Canadians directly into businesses" while helping small and medium-sized firms adopt AI. Mitacs already partners with more than 200 post-secondary institutions, so universities will largely reach these placements through arrangements they already have.
