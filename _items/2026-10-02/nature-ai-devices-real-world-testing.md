A Nature editorial argues that AI-powered medical devices that inform clinical decisions should be tested in real-world settings, applying the principles behind drug trials even where a full-scale randomized controlled trial is not needed. It cites an analysis finding that only 23% of some 4,600 papers on AI tools for clinical medicine used real-world patient data, and only 19 were prospective randomized trials. The editorial notes that manufacturers can certify some AI products without any real-world assessment, and calls for data transparency, open data, and real-world testing to be non-negotiable.
