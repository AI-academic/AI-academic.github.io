A Daily Nous post reviewing how publishers govern reviewers' use of AI finds no common standard. Springer Nature frames the question as "not whether AI is used, but how" and uses a risk-assessment framework; Wiley permits responsible use with disclosure but bars uploading confidential manuscripts to AI systems; Oxford University Press prohibits uploading manuscripts to generative AI tools for any purpose, and the University of Chicago Press bars AI tools in writing reviewers' reports. Many leading philosophy journals give reviewers no explicit guidance at all. The post also notes that editors may lack the means to detect violations, and that journals' rules on the finality of decisions can leave authors no way to contest a review they suspect was AI-written.
