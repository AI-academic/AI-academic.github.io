Pew Research Center tested whether AI models can stand in for human survey respondents, the "silicon samples" approach now appearing in social-science methods. Across nearly 300 questions, the synthetic answers differed from human answers by an average of 12.4 percentage points, with errors above 15 points on roughly 28% of questions. The AI poll often took views common within a political subgroup and portrayed them as nearly universal, and nearly half the questions had at least one answer option that no AI respondent chose, suggesting a narrow range of responses. It is hard to believe that humans may be replaced by AI when it comes to collecting survey responses.
