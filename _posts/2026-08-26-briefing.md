## Ontario institutions

### Waterloo-led study finds current open-weight model safeguards vulnerable to tampering

- Waterloo published an institutional account of TamperBench, a standardized framework used to evaluate 21 open-weight large language models across nine tampering threats. The underlying paper reports that current alignment-stage defences largely failed to withstand systematic attack sweeps. Waterloo said the work was recently presented at the ACM Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining. The paper itself was first submitted in February and revised in June; the new development in this briefing window is Waterloo's August 25 release following the conference presentation. **Read here:** [Major security weaknesses found in leading open AI models; TamperBench: Systematically Stress-Testing LLM Safety Under Fine-Tuning and Tampering](https://uwaterloo.ca/news/media/major-security-weaknesses-found-leading-open-ai-models)

## Government updates

### UNESCO reports a September ministerial statement and new evidence on AI in education

- UNESCO announced that Digital Learning Week, September 8-11 in Paris, will bring together more than 25 education ministers and other participants to adopt a joint ministerial statement on sustaining education as a common good in the age of AI. UNESCO also plans to release policy briefs on AI safety, sovereignty, procurement, cost models, and age-appropriateness, along with new survey data on AI trends in higher education. The announcement establishes the agenda and planned outputs; their substance is not yet available for assessment. **Read here:** [Education in the age of AI: Ministerial statement to be adopted at UNESCO's Digital Learning Week](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/education-age-ai-ministerial-statement-be-adopted-unescos-digital-learning-week)

## Other notable updates

### MIT releases a final report calling for institution-wide, continuously adapting AI education practices

- MIT's president and senior academic leaders released the final report of the Ad Hoc Committee on AI Use in Teaching, Learning, and Research Training. The committee recommends three broad directions: create "AI-aware" educational processes, including changes to learning goals, assessment, and policy; centre people, community, and the residential experience; and build processes, teams, and tools for continuous reflection and improvement. MIT also called for clear course-level AI-use policies, renewed emphasis on hands-on learning, guidance and pilot funding, and discipline-specific communities of practice for teaching and research. **Read here:** [AI and education: A watershed moment for MIT](https://orgchart.mit.edu/letters/ai-and-education-watershed-moment-mit)

### Study finds LLM summaries can intensify unsupported causal claims in social science research

- In a peer-reviewed study published August 24 and captured in today's short look-back, researchers analyzed 194,631 cross-sectional social-science articles using an LLM-based workflow with human validation. They reported that 46% contained causal language in titles or abstracts across 1980-2024, rising from 20% in 2000 to 60% in 2024. Experiments with five LLMs found that summaries could remove hedges or introduce unqualified causal claims, while prompts emphasizing caution reduced the effect. The authors also found that methodological labels and associational wording reduced causal interpretation among human readers. **Read here:** [Quantifying the prevalence and impact of overreaching causal claims in social science](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02553-x)
