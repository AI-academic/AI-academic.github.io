## Canadian academic institution updates (non-Ontario)

### Dalhousie launches the Atlantic AI Institute

- Dalhousie announced the Senate-approved Atlantic AI Institute (A2I2), succeeding and broadening work begun through its Institute for Big Data Analytics. The institute launches with about 50 core members across 10 faculties and units, with more than 100 affiliated students and postdoctoral researchers. Its three pillars are research, academics, and community; planned activities include academic programs, microcredentials, mentorship, partnerships, and commercialization support.

[Dalhousie launches Atlantic AI Institute to help shape responsible AI in the region](https://www.dal.ca/news/2026/08/20/atlantic-ai-institute-dalhousie.html)


## Updates from other, non-academic sectors

### Google offers eligible university students one year of paid-tier Gemini access at no charge

- Google announced one-year student offers for its paid AI plans - Google AI Pro for eligible U.S. college students and Google AI Plus for eligible students in selected markets outside the United States. The offer includes higher Gemini limits and student-oriented features such as study notebooks, quizzes, deadline extraction from syllabi, interactive visualizations, and asynchronous Deep Research. The terms require eligibility verification and, for some offers, a payment method; plans may convert to paid subscriptions if not cancelled.

[Start the semester with one year of Gemini, on us](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/student-offer-google-ai/)

## Other notable updates

### U.S. accreditor adopts foundational AI principles

- HLC adopted seven principles for its own use and integration of AI: integrity; transparency; mission and strategic alignment; guidance; responsible efficiency; accountability; and privacy and security. HLC also said it will form an advisory group and develop resources for member institutions, including guidance related to accreditation and accreditation standards.

[HLC Sets AI Principles to Guide its Operations and Activities](https://www.hlcommission.org/learning-center/news/hlc-sets-ai-principles-to-guide-its-operations-and-activities/)

### Delaware State announces its role in a US$21.5 million NSF human-AI institute

- Delaware State University announced its participation in the five-year, US$21.5-million NSF AI Institute for Human-AI Cooperation, led by the University of Delaware with the University of Pennsylvania, Princeton University, and the University of Florida. Delaware State will contribute research and infrastructure, lead engagement with 13 historically Black colleges and universities, and help develop internships and summer AI camps focused on health care and rehabilitation.

[Delaware State University partners in $21.5 million NSF AI Institute](www.desu.edu/news/2026/08/delaware-state-university-partners-215-million-nsf-ai-institute)

### Scrutiny of AI-assisted scholarly writing and peer review

- A Nature Computational Science editorial reiterated that AI may assist with language, structure, formatting, and translation, but must not replace scholarly judgment; it also emphasized disclosure, human accountability, and the prohibition on uploading confidential review materials to public or unsecured AI systems. Separately, Nature reported on emerging AI-text detection methods and on an unreviewed preprint estimating widespread signs of AI-assisted writing in recent biomedical papers.

[Responsible and transparent use of AI in scientific publishing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01043-4)
[Staggering 90% of biomedical papers now show signs of AI help](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02551-z)
