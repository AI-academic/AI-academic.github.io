## Ontario institution updates

### McMaster secures $43 million for five research chairs and five emerging leaders

McMaster announced that five researchers from U.S. universities will join as Eddie Goldenberg Research Chairs of Canada, each supported by $8 million over eight years, and that the university received five Canada Impact+ Emerging Leaders awards totalling $3 million over six years. The chairs are expected to arrive between September 2026 and July 2027, subject to McMaster governance approvals. Their programs span radiopharmaceuticals, musculoskeletal health, sustainable innovation, and advanced materials; James LeBeau's chair will use quantitative and AI-enabled electron microscopy to study materials for batteries, semiconductors, and quantum devices. **Read here:** [McMaster University, institutional announcement](https://degroote.mcmaster.ca/articles/mcmaster-recruits-five-top-scholars-from-american-universities/)

### Waterloo establishes a $500,000 professorship for AI-driven water-risk research

The University of Waterloo reported that an alumnus's $500,000 investment established the Sinnathamby Professorship in AI for Sustainable Solutions. John Quilty's graduate research team is developing machine-learning models for river-flow forecasting, reservoir operations, drinking-water contamination, and rare high-impact floods. Waterloo says the support enabled student recruitment and that results are already contributing to a flood-forecasting partnership with Natural Resources Canada. **Read here:** [Alum's investment buoys AI-driven water risk research](https://uwaterloo.ca/engineering/news/alums-investment-buoys-ai-driven-water-risk-research)

## Updates from other, non-academic sectors

### Randomized study separates gains from ChatGPT access and causal-reasoning training

Bocconi University and OpenAI researchers randomly assigned 1,053 first-year economics, management, and finance students to receive ChatGPT Edu access, causal-reasoning training, both, or neither while completing a business case. ChatGPT access increased the standard evaluation score by an estimated 0.86 points on a five-point scale and produced more expert-like responses. Causal-reasoning training increased the diversity of ideas and the use of mechanisms and falsifiability, but those qualities were not rewarded by the standard rubric. The authors found that combining the interventions retained the distinct benefits of each. This is an open working paper, not a peer-reviewed publication, and the experiment involved one assignment at one university.

**Read here:** [Better answers, broader thinking: What students gain from ChatGPT and critical-thinking training](https://openai.com/index/what-students-gain-from-chatgpt-critical-thinking-training/)

## Government updates

### U.S. National Science Foundation commits $90 million to three research centres

The U.S. National Science Foundation announced a five-year, $90 million investment in three new Science and Technology Centers, each expected to receive about $6 million annually during the initial term and potentially compete for five additional years. The Michigan State-led TEMPEST centre will combine experiments, high-performance computing, mathematical modelling, and AI to build physics-grounded turbulence models. The University of Texas at Austin-led Center for Human and Robot Co-Adaptation will combine robotics, AI, and human factors to study secure, effective adaptation between people and embodied systems. A third, Northwestern-led centre will focus on three-dimensional genome organization and engineering. **Read here:** [NSF launches three new Science and Technology Centers with $90M investment](https://www.nsf.gov/news/nsf-launches-three-new-science-technology-centers-90m)

## Other notable updates

### University of Colorado establishes a systemwide responsible-use policy for AI

University of Colorado Administrative Policy Statement was announced on August 26. It establishes campus and systemwide AI committees and requires review when an AI tool processes university data, integrates with university systems, or significantly shapes decisions affecting individuals. It also requires disclosure when generative AI contributes to high-impact official outputs and holds human users accountable for reviewing outputs. The policy preserves faculty's principal responsibility for pedagogy, curriculum, research, scholarly work, and academic ethics, and leaves coursework guidance to academic units through shared governance. **Read here:** [Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence | University of Colorado policy alert](https://www.cu.edu/ope/aps/6012)

### UCL reports first live use of its AI system during sight-saving brain surgery

University College London reported that an in-house AI system analyzed a live endoscopic video feed during pituitary-tumour surgery and highlighted critical anatomy for the surgical team. The procedure was the first patient use in an ongoing clinical trial; UCL says the tumour was removed and the patient's vision improved. The system was trained and evaluated on annotated surgical videos and ran on an NVIDIA Clara IGX platform. Evidence released so far is an institutional account of one patient, not peer-reviewed trial results. **Read here:** [First patient in live AI-assisted sight-saving brain surgery](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2026/aug/first-patient-live-ai-assisted-sight-saving-brain-surgery)
